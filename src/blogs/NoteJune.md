## AY reflection 

#### Ryan Zhang

#### August 7, 2025

#### Teaching

The past academic year started when I taught an introductory machine learning course for the third/fourth time.
Compared to previous year's offering, I made several changes to the course structure:
- First, I decided to move up the course project proposal schedule several weeks earlier.
- Second, I added two lectures to introduce the basics of foundation models, in particular, how probabilistic models generate text, how fine-tuning and in-context learning works.
- Lastly, I added plenty of examples throughout the lectures to better explain the maths behind machine learning.

During the spring semester, I decided to write down the lectures notes I've been preparing for my advanced machine learning course. This has turned out to be a major success, as many students appreciate the detailed notes that serve as a complement to the in-person lectures.

#### Research

At the beginning of September, one program director that we've been interacting informed us that our project on multitask and fine-tuning has been approved for funding.
This is a great news as we've been working in this direction for several years, and we've developed a linear approximation framework for scaling multitask learning on many tasks trained with foundation models.
The funding we received will allow us to expand the impact of this research and further apply the ideas we've developed to different types of applications and problems.
It will also allow us to recruit more students and involve them in the graduate training.

During the middle of the year, there has been news that the NSF will cut down its research funding by more than one half.
This has created some confusion and panic around what would happen next.
However, this seems to have been reverted so that is really fortunate news for the community.

Another major change that has happend this year is the extensive use of LLMs for doing research. I have found LLMs particularly useful while doing tedious calculations and checking related works. In this age where a lot of the research is assisted by AI agents, it seems all the more important to have the ability to conduct independent, creative research in this exciting time.

It was also a great pleasure to attend JSM for the first time; I had been wanting to attend the conference after the pandemic. It was great to finally turn around, and it is truly inspring to hear talks from legends in the field.

#### Looking forward

The past academic year has been an extremely challenging one. Nevertheless, we remain excited about the research that we're pursuing moving forward.
Two of the main focus we're exploring now involve:
- Developing computational methods for understanding neural networks, by measuring Hessian statistics.
- Better understanding agents, including (algorithmic) reasoning, reinforcement learning.

Lastly, thanks to all of the colleagues who have interacted with us and who have helped contribute to the development our research.
We are excited about the next academic year.
