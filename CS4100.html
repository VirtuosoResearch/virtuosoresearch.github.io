CS4100: Artificial Intelligence (Fall 2025)

Course overview

This course provides an introduction to the core principles of artificial intelligence, covering key areas such as search, decision-making under uncertainty, probabilistic graphical models for reasoning, and machine learning. While these topics represent only a fraction of the entire field, students will, by the end of the course, gain a broad set of technical tools that will allow them to address real-world problems, continue self-study of new methods, and take advanced courses in AI. The emphasis will be on problem formulations, models, and algorithms. Applications will be presented when appropriate, but they are not the main focus. In line with experiential learning, students will engage in substantial hands-on practice through programming assignments and the final project.

This class will present a number of important modeling and prediction techniques that are staples in the fields of machine learning, artificial intelligence, and data science. In addition, this course will cover the statistical underpinnings of the methodology. The tentative list of topics includes:

  • Regression and classification as a predictive task and general model fitting: a review of linear regression, cross-validation, bootstrapping, sparse regression (Ridge, LASSO), tree-based methods.

  • Neural networks and deep learning: convolutional neural networks, backpropagation, transformer neural networks, language modeling.

  • Causality, reasoning, inference: potential outcomes, inverse propensity weighting, matching, difference-in-difference.

  • Unsupervised learning: Principal component analysis, clustering.

Course syllabus

Week 1, Sep 4: Course introduction

  • Examples about linear regression, image classification and language modeling (naive Bayes).

Week 2, Sep 8: Machine learning, Sep 11: Linear regression

  • The bias-variance tradeoff in supervised learning, learning polynomial functions, and K-nearest neighbhors.

  • Simple linear regression, multiple linear regression, with some review of linear algebra.

Week 3, Sep 15: Logistic regression, Sep 18: Neural networks and backpropagation

  • Logistic function, logistic loss (log-loss), maximum likelihood estimation, and cross-entropy loss.

  • Neural networks, non-linearpredictors, and backpropagation.

Week 4, Sep 22: Generalization, Sep 25: Language models and generative models

  • Overfitting and generalization.

  • Transformers, language models, generative models, and low-rank adaptation.

Week 5, Sep 29: Searching, Oct 2: Search and heuristics

  • BFS, DFS, dynamic programming, and uniform cost search.

  • A-star and heuristics.

Week 6, Oct 6: Markov decision processes (MDPs), Oct 9: Bellman equations

  • Policy evaluation, value iteration, and Q-learning.

  • Bellman equations.

Week 7, Oct 16: Games I

  • Game evaluation, expectimax, minimax, expectiminimax, and Alpha-beta pruning.

Week 8, Coursework project presentation

  • Note: In class attendance required.

Week 9: Oct 27: Games II, Oct 30: Reinforcement learning

  • TD learning, simultaneous games, and none-zero-sum games.

  • Reinforcement learning, RLHF, reward model, and preference optimization.

Week 10, Nov 3: Uncertainty, knowledge, and reasoning, Nov 6: Bayesian networks I

  • Supervised learning, smoothing, and EM algorithm.

Week 11, Nov 10: Bayesian networks II, Nov 13: Hidden Markov models

Week 12, Nov 17: Midterm review, Nov 22: Midterm exam

  • Note: The exam is take home.

Week 13, Nov 24: Conclusion and looking forward

Week 14, Dec 1: Final project presentation, Dec 4: Guest lecture.

  • Note: In class attendance required.
